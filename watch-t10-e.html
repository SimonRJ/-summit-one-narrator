A square of dots grows from three rows of three to four rows of four. How many dots are added? And what is the rule for growing any square to the next one?

Keep the nine original dots. Add a new bottom row. The new row belongs to the bigger square, so it has four dots.

Now add the new right-hand column. Its bottom dot is already there, as part of the new row. So only three more dots are needed.

Four plus three is seven dots added. Nine plus seven is sixteen, which is four times four.

The same picture works for any size. To grow a square of side n, add a row of n plus one dots and a column of n dots. That is two n plus one dots. So from ten by ten to eleven by eleven you add twenty-one dots.

That is why the gaps between square numbers are the odd numbers: three, five, seven, nine. Rebuild this argument, then answer: how many dots are added when a twenty by twenty square grows to twenty-one by twenty-one?
