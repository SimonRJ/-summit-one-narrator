T04.1

Factor with a purpose

Break a number into primes, and know when to stop searching for factors.

1:59 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Write 84 as a product of primes.

Check your reasoning

Split 84 into 12 × 7. Then 12 = 3 × 4 and 4 = 2 × 2. The leaves are all prime, so 84 = 2² × 3 × 7. Multiplying them gives 4 × 21 = 84.

Try a variation

Use prime factors to find the smallest whole number greater than 1 that divides both 90 and 105.

See the worked answer

The prime factors are 90 = 2 × 3² × 5 and 105 = 3 × 5 × 7. They share 3 and 5. The smallest common divisor above 1 is 3.

Read the transcript

0:00 Split 60 into 6 and 10

Whole numbers bigger than one are made by multiplying primes. A prime has exactly two positive factors, one and itself. Take sixty. Split it into six and ten, then split those again. The prime pieces are two, three, two and five.

0:18 Start again with 4 and 15

Start another way. Write sixty as four times fifteen. Four gives two twos, and fifteen gives a three and a five. Rearrange the pieces in increasing order and both trees give the same list. That fixed prime factorisation is why we can work with the pieces.

0:35 Choose primes to test against 91

Is ninety-one prime? Decide which small primes you would try first, and think about how far the search needs to go.

0:46 Find the factor that divides 91

Ninety-one is odd, so two fails. Its digits add to ten, so three fails. Its last digit is one, so five fails. Try seven. Seven times thirteen equals ninety-one. These two factors show that ninety-one is composite.

1:03 Decide when the search for a factor of 97 ends

If both factors were bigger than the square root, multiplying them would give too much. So a composite number has a prime factor at or below its square root. For ninety-seven that root lies between nine and ten. Try two, three, five and seven. All leave a remainder, so ninety-seven is prime.

1:22 Share the prime factors of 66 among three numbers

Three different whole numbers, each bigger than one, multiply to sixty-six. Which numbers are they? Sixty-six has exactly three prime pieces, two, three and eleven. Each of the three numbers needs a piece. Each gets exactly one, so those three primes are the answer.

1:40 Build a factor tree for 84

Split a number until all its pieces are prime. When you test whether a number is prime, use the small primes up to its square root. One has only one positive factor. In your notebook, write eighty-four as a product of primes.
