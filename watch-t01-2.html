T01.2

Choose a representation that exposes the relationship

Choose a line, table, sketch or equation that reveals the relationship.

2:51 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Trees are planted every 4 m along a 40 m path, including both ends. How many trees are there?

Check your reasoning

40 ÷ 4 = 10 gaps. The starting tree plus one at the end of each gap makes 11 trees.

Try a variation

Fence posts are 5 m apart around a closed 30 m loop. How many posts are needed?

See the worked answer

There are 30 ÷ 5 = 6 gaps. Walk around the loop and count the post at the start of each gap. That counts each post once, so there are 6.

Read the transcript

0:00 Choose a line, table or sketch

A difficult-looking question can become much clearer as soon as you draw the right picture. A line shows order. A table sets comparisons side by side. A sketch makes shapes and boundaries visible. An equation holds a relationship in place. Choose the one that helps you see what the question is asking.

Picture a fence. Your eye can follow every post or every panel between two posts. A sketch gives both counts a place on the same line. The words each end tell us where the fence starts and stops. Mark those ends first. Then look for what repeats between them.

0:36 Place posts along a straight fence

A straight fence is thirty metres long. There is a post at each end, and the posts are five metres apart. How many posts are there? Draw the full thirty metres. Mark the left end as zero, and mark off spaces of five metres.

Thirty metres divided into five-metre spaces gives six gaps. Follow each gap to its right-hand end. That accounts for six posts. The post at zero sits before the first gap, so add it once. Six gaps, plus that starting post, makes seven posts.

1:10 Join the ends of the fence

Now join the ends to make a closed fence, using the same six equal panels. Watch the last post come back to the very first one. They meet at a single corner. Each panel now ends at a different post, and all posts are included. That makes six gaps and six posts.

1:28 Count Sam once in the queue

Try the same kind of line with a queue. Sam is ninth from the front and sixth from the back. How many people are there? Eight stand in front of Sam, and five stand behind. Eight, plus Sam, plus five, makes fourteen. Both ranks include Sam, so give Sam one place in the picture.

1:47 Compare the price per pencil

A table helps when there are several things to compare. Box A contains eight pencils and costs sixteen dollars. Box B contains four pencils and costs twelve dollars. Add a price per pencil column. The divisions give two dollars and three dollars. Box A costs more for the box and less for each pencil.

2:06 Trace the outside edges of two mats

A sketch reveals a boundary. Join two mats, each four metres by three metres, along their three-metre sides. How much edge is exposed? Each mat has fourteen metres around it. The pair has twenty-eight before joining. The shared edge removes three metres from each mat, so the equation gives twenty-two metres outside.
