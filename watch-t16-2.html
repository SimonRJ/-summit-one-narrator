T16.2

Convert time descriptions into rates

Turn "takes so long" into "does so much each minute", and add rates.

2:00 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

One painter can paint a wall in 4 hours and another can paint the same wall in 12 hours. They start together and work at their usual constant rates without getting in each other’s way. How long does one wall take?

Check your reasoning

In one hour the painters complete 1/4 + 1/12 = 4/12 = 1/3 of the wall. At that combined rate, the whole wall takes 3 hours.

Try a variation

One tap fills a tank in 8 minutes. A drain empties a full tank in 24 minutes. Both operate at constant rates together. How long does it take to fill an empty tank?

See the worked answer

In one minute the tap adds 1/8 of the tank and the drain removes 1/24. The net rate is 3/24 − 1/24 = 1/12 of a tank per minute. One full tank takes 12 minutes.

Read the transcript

0:00 Convert ten minutes per tank into a rate

If a tap fills a tank in ten minutes, then each minute it fills one tenth of the tank. Turning a time into an amount per minute is the key move. Amounts per minute can be added together. Times cannot.

0:13 Combine the water delivered in one minute

When two taps run together at constant rates, the tank fills faster. The two amounts filled in one minute can be added. That combined amount per minute tells us how long the whole job will take.

0:25 Start a six-minute tap and a three-minute tap

One tap fills a tank in six minutes. Another fills it in three minutes. Both run at constant rates. How long do they take together? Pause and work out what each tap does in one minute.

0:40 Model each tap with sixths of a tank

Picture the tank in six equal slices. In one minute, the slow tap fills one slice. The fast tap fills the whole tank in three minutes, so it fills two slices a minute. Together they fill three slices a minute.

0:55 Check the time for both taps together

Six slices at three slices a minute takes two minutes. Together the taps take two minutes, which is less than either alone, as it should be.

1:04 Subtract a leak from the filling rate

Rates can also work against each other. A tap fills a tank in four minutes, while a leak would empty it in twelve. In a tank of twelve slices, the tap adds three slices a minute and the leak removes one. The net gain is two slices a minute, so the tank fills in six minutes.

1:22 Close a 100 m gap between two runners

Moving objects follow the same rule. Two runners start one hundred metres apart and run towards each other, at six metres per second and four metres per second. The gap closes at ten metres per second. They meet after ten seconds.

1:38 Combine the rates of two painters

Convert each time into a rate. Use the same time unit for each rate. Add rates that help and subtract rates that hinder. Divide the job by the combined rate. Notebook challenge: one painter paints a fence in four hours, another in twelve hours. How long do they take together?
