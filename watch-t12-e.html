Worked example: the angle outside a triangle.

Read the transcript

0:00 Locate the exterior angle at C

Triangle A B C has an angle of forty-eight degrees at A and sixty-seven degrees at B. The side B C is extended beyond C to a point D. Find the angle A C D, the angle outside the triangle.

0:19 Find the inside angle using 48° and 67°

First find the angle inside the triangle at C. The three angles total one hundred and eighty. One hundred and eighty minus forty-eight minus sixty-seven is sixty-five degrees.

0:31 Follow the extension from B through C

B, C and D lie on a straight line. The inside angle and the outside angle at C together make one hundred and eighty. So the outside angle is one hundred and eighty minus sixty-five: one hundred and fifteen degrees.

0:45 Compare the exterior angle with the two far angles

Notice that forty-eight plus sixty-seven is also one hundred and fifteen. That is no accident. The outside angle of a triangle always equals the sum of the two inside angles furthest from it.

0:58 Test the shortcut with 60° and 55°

Check with a different triangle. If the far angles were sixty and fifty-five, the inside angle at C would be sixty-five again, and the outside angle one hundred and fifteen. The shortcut and the long method agree.

1:12 Use 35° and 80° to find the exterior angle

The outside angle equals the sum of the two far inside angles. Rebuild this argument, then find the outside angle when the far angles are thirty-five and eighty degrees.
