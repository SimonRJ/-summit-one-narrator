T03.1

Read a remainder as a precise statement

Read "remainder r on division by d" as: the number is r more than a multiple of d.

2:16 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Today is Tuesday. What day will it be 100 days from now?

Check your reasoning

100 = 7 x 14 + 2, so two days after Tuesday: Thursday.

Read the transcript

0:00 Group seventeen counters in fives

Seventeen counters make three full groups of five, with two left over. That leftover is called the remainder. The three full groups give a multiple of five. The two counters tell you exactly how far seventeen is past that multiple.

When we divide whole numbers by five, the possible remainders are zero, one, two, three and four. A leftover of five would make another complete group. However large the starting number, it fits into one of these five remainder classes.

0:31 Mark the four-bead cycle

A necklace repeats red, blue, green and yellow in that order. The first bead is red. What colour is bead fifty? Try marking the boundaries between the repeating groups of four.

0:46 Locate bead 50

A full cycle has four beads. Fifty is twelve groups of four, plus two more beads. The twelve groups take us to bead forty-eight. Count two beads into the next cycle. Its second colour is blue, so bead fifty is blue.

1:01 Place bead 48 at remainder zero

Bead forty-eight lands on a complete group boundary. It has remainder zero when divided by four. In this position list we count from one, so a complete group finishes on its fourth bead. Bead forty-eight is yellow.

1:16 Subtract two numbers with the same remainder

Twenty-three is four groups of five plus three. Thirty-eight is seven groups of five plus three. In the difference, the two leftovers cancel. Three extra groups of five give fifteen. And adding any whole group of five leaves a remainder unchanged. That is why equal remainders and a multiple-of-five difference go together.

Try combining two conditions. Below thirty, the numbers with remainder three on division by five are three, eight, thirteen, eighteen, twenty-three and twenty-eight. Now test them for remainder one on division by three. Only thirteen and twenty-eight reach that column.
