T03.2

Separate the first coincidence from the repeat period

Find when two repeating events first coincide, and how often after that.

2:04 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Buses leave every 8 minutes and every 12 minutes, both at 9:00. When do they next leave together?

Check your reasoning

The lowest common multiple of 8 and 12 is 24, so at 9:24.

Try a variation

A blue light flashes at minutes 2, 8, 14 and every 6 minutes thereafter. A red light flashes at minutes 5, 14, 23 and every 9 minutes thereafter. What are their first two shared flash times?

See the worked answer

They first meet at minute 14. After that their shared times repeat every 18 minutes, the smallest common multiple of 6 and 9. The next shared time is minute 32.

Read the transcript

0:00 Compare the four- and six-second lights

Two lights repeat at different speeds. One flashes every four seconds and the other every six. We can ask when they first flash together after starting. We can also ask how much time separates one meeting from the next.

Twenty-four is a multiple of both four and six. A smaller common multiple may also work. First mark where each light starts. Then add its own period each time. A timeline shows both the starting positions and the repeated jumps.

0:31 Start both lights at zero

Both lights flash at time zero. One repeats every four seconds. The other repeats every six seconds. When do they next flash together? List a few times for each light, and line up the lists.

0:47 Find the gap between shared flashes

The first light flashes at four, eight, twelve and sixteen. The second flashes at six, twelve and eighteen. Their first positive meeting is at twelve seconds. Twelve is the lowest common multiple of four and six. A gap between meetings has to be a multiple of both periods, so the next meeting is twelve seconds later.

1:08 Give the second light a two-second delay

Let the second light start at time two. Its times are two, eight, fourteen and twenty. The first light's times stay on the multiples of four. The first meeting moves to eight seconds, and the next is at twenty. The starting offset changed the first meeting. The twelve-second gap stayed the same.

1:26 Use parity to rule out a meeting

Now start the second light at time one. Its jumps of six keep all its times odd. Starting from zero and moving in jumps of four keeps the other times even. An odd time and an even time cannot match, so these two lights never flash together.

1:42 Find the next shared bus departure

When meetings are possible, the lowest common multiple gives the gap between them. The starting positions decide when the first meeting happens. In your notebook, one bus leaves every eight minutes and another every twelve. They both leave at nine o'clock. When do they next leave together?
