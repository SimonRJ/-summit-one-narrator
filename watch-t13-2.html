T13.2

Separate boundary change from scale change

Tell apart changes to a boundary and changes of scale.

1:58 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Every length of a photograph is tripled. How many times as large is its area?

Check your reasoning

3 x 3 = 9 times.

Try a variation

A 4 cm by 7 cm rectangle is enlarged by a scale factor of 2. How do its perimeter and area change?

See the worked answer

The enlarged sides are 8 cm and 14 cm. The perimeter doubles from 22 cm to 44 cm. The area grows from 28 cm² to 112 cm², four times as large.

Read the transcript

0:00 Predict how a cut changes the boundary

Cutting a piece out of a shape always reduces its area. But the perimeter might stay the same, or even grow. Area and perimeter respond to changes in completely different ways.

0:13 Choose a measure for a cut or an enlargement

Questions often ask about perimeter after a cut, or area after an enlargement. Each needs its own reasoning, and mixing them up is a common mistake.

0:22 Cut a unit square from a corner

A five by three rectangle has a one by one square cut from a corner. What is its perimeter now? Pause and compare the old boundary with the new.

0:35 Match the two lost edges to the two new edges

The original perimeter is sixteen. The cut removes two short pieces of boundary, each of length one. But it exposes two new pieces, also each of length one. The perimeter is still sixteen. The new edges can be slid out to replace the old ones exactly.

0:52 Move the cut to the middle of a side

Now cut the same square from the middle of a side instead. One piece of length one is removed, but three new pieces appear. The perimeter rises by two, to eighteen. Same area removed, different effect on the boundary.

1:07 Double the sides of a two-by-three rectangle

Scaling is a different kind of change. Double every length of a two by three rectangle and you get four by six. The perimeter doubles, from ten to twenty. But the area becomes four times as big, from six to twenty-four, because both the length and the width were doubled.

1:24 Double or triple the edges of a cube

For solids, three directions are scaled. Doubling every length of a cube makes its volume eight times as large. Tripling makes it twenty-seven times as large.

1:35 Scale a photograph by a factor of three

For a cut, compare the boundary removed with the boundary created. For scaling by a factor, lengths multiply by the factor, areas by its square, and volumes by its cube. Notebook challenge: a photograph is enlarged so that every length is three times as long. How many times as large is its area?
