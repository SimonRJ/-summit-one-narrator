T08.3

Recount within the information you are given

When given information, cross out impossible outcomes and recount.

1:49 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

A fair six-sided die numbered 1 to 6 has been rolled. You learn that the result is greater than 2. What is the probability that it is odd?

Check your reasoning

The clue leaves four equally likely results, 3, 4, 5 and 6. Two of them are odd, so the conditional probability is 2/4 = 1/2.

Try a variation

Two fair coins are tossed independently. You learn that at least one is a head. What is the probability that both are heads?

See the worked answer

Before the information, HH, HT, TH and TT are equally likely. Only TT is excluded. Of the three remaining ordered outcomes, one is HH, giving probability 1/3.

Read the transcript

0:00 Cross out outcomes the clue rules out

New information changes a probability. When you are told something about the result, cross out every outcome that no longer fits. What is left is your new, smaller world, and you count inside it.

0:13 Find the total after filtering the list

The usual mistake is to keep the old total on the bottom of the fraction. After information arrives, the total is the number of outcomes that survive.

0:22 Roll an even number on a fair die

A fair die is rolled. You are told the result is even. What is the probability that it is greater than three? Pause and cross out what cannot have happened.

0:35 Keep only the even faces

The die shows one to six. "Even" removes one, three and five. Three outcomes survive: two, four and six. They are still equally likely.

0:45 Count faces above three in the reduced list

Of those three, the numbers greater than three are four and six. So the probability is two out of three. Without the information it would have been three out of six, one half. The information changed the answer.

0:59 List the dice pairs that total eight

Roll two fair dice separately. You are told the total is eight. Five pairs survive: two and six, three and five, four and four, five and three, six and two. What is the chance that both dice show the same number? Only four and four works: one out of five.

1:18 Check whether the survivors are equally likely

Crossing out works because outcomes that were equally likely before the information are still equally likely afterwards. If the outcomes are not equally likely, you compare their probabilities rather than just counting them.

1:31 Use the clue that a roll exceeded two

Cross out what the information rules out. Then count favourable outcomes among the survivors. Notebook challenge: a die is rolled and you are told the number is greater than two. What is the probability that it is odd?
