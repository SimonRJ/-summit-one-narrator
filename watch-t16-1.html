T16.1

Turn a ratio into equal parts

Treat a ratio as a number of equal parts, and find the size of one part.

1:51 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Red and blue beads are in the ratio 5 : 3, with 16 more red than blue. How many beads altogether?

Check your reasoning

Five red parts minus three blue parts leaves two parts. Those two parts contain 16 beads, so each part contains 8. There are eight parts altogether, giving 8 × 8 = 64 beads. Check the shares of 40 red and 24 blue differ by 16.

Read the transcript

0:00 Interpret three parts for every two

A ratio of three to two means three equal parts of one thing for every two equal parts of another. The numbers three and two are not the amounts. They count parts. Find the size of one part, and everything else follows.

0:15 Find the value of one equal part

Ratios run through recipes, maps, mixtures and sharing problems. Nearly all of them are solved by the same step: work out what one part is worth.

0:25 Share forty stickers in the ratio 3 to 2

Forty stickers are shared between Ana and Ben in the ratio three to two. How many does each get? Pause and count the parts.

0:36 Distribute five equal parts between Ana and Ben

There are three plus two parts: five altogether. Forty stickers in five equal parts is eight per part. Ana gets three parts, twenty-four. Ben gets two parts, sixteen.

0:49 Use a difference of nine instead of a total

The given fact is not always the total. Suppose instead you are told that Ana has nine more than Ben. Ana has one more part than Ben, so one part is nine. Then Ana has twenty-seven and Ben has eighteen.

1:03 Match B before joining two ratios

To join two ratios, make the shared quantity match. If A to B is two to three, and B to C is four to five, rewrite both so that B is twelve parts. Then A to B to C is eight to twelve to fifteen.

1:19 Express the first share as a fraction of the whole

Ratios and fractions are two views of one picture. In the ratio three to two, the first share is three fifths of the whole, not three halves and not two thirds.

1:30 Recover the total from sixteen extra red beads

Count the parts. Use what you are told, a total, a difference or one share, to find one part. Then multiply. Notebook challenge: red and blue beads are in the ratio five to three, and there are sixteen more red than blue. How many beads are there altogether?
