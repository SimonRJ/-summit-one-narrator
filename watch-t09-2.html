T09.2

Read decimal places as divisibility conditions

Connect decimals to fractions with tens, and predict when a decimal stops.

2:00 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Which of 7/20, 5/12 and 9/75 have decimal expansions that terminate?

Check your reasoning

7/20 = 35/100 = 0.35. The reduced fraction 5/12 has a factor of 3 in the denominator, so it cannot be written with a power of 10 as its denominator. Reduce the last fraction first. It becomes 3/25 = 12/100 = 0.12.

Read the transcript

A decimal that stops is just a fraction whose bottom number is ten, a hundred, a thousand, and so on. Nought point three seven five is three hundred and seventy-five thousandths. And every power of ten is built from twos and fives only.

That one fact tells you which fractions make decimals that stop, and which go on forever, without doing any long division.

Does three fortieths give a decimal that stops? And what is it? Pause and factorise forty.

Forty is two times two times two times five. To make a power of ten, every two needs a five. There are three twos and one five, so supply two more fives. Multiply top and bottom by twenty-five. Three fortieths becomes seventy-five thousandths: nought point nought seven five.

Now try one sixth. Six is two times three. No amount of multiplying by twos and fives can turn that three into part of a power of ten. So one sixth never stops: nought point one six six six, forever.

One warning: simplify first. Six fifteenths has a three in the bottom, but it cancels. Six fifteenths is two fifths, which is nought point four. Only the simplified fraction tells the truth.

Decimals also hide divisibility. Nought point two five is a quarter, so multiplying by four gives a whole number. A decimal with two places always becomes whole when multiplied by one hundred, and sometimes by something smaller.

Simplify the fraction. If the bottom is built only from twos and fives, the decimal stops. If any other prime remains, it repeats forever. Notebook challenge: which of these stop: seven twentieths, five twelfths, nine seventy-fifths?
