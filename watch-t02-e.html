Follow what changes and what stays fixed

Worked example: why one order always wins by the same amount.

1:44 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Find the gap between "add 2 then multiply by 6" and "multiply by 6 then add 2".

Check your reasoning

6x + 12 and 6x + 2; the gap is always 10.

Try a variation

One sequence adds 4 then triples. A second triples then adds a number. What must that number be for the outputs always to match?

See the worked answer

The first output is 3(n + 4) = 3n + 12. The second sequence must add 12. Testing a new input, such as 5, gives 27 by either route.

Read the transcript

0:00 Try both rules with one starting number

Start with any whole number, including zero. Rule A adds three and then multiplies by four. Rule B multiplies by four and then adds three. Which output is larger, and by how much? Sketch one example before looking for a proof.

0:19 Make four copies of the sum

Call the start x. Rule A forms x plus three and makes four copies of the whole amount. Look at one copy. It contains an x tile and a three tile. Four copies give four x tiles and four threes, so the result is four x plus twelve.

In Rule B, the multiplication acts on the starting value. Make four copies of x. Now add the three. This time the three comes in once, after the copying step. The result is four x plus three.

0:50 Cancel the matching tiles

Line up the outputs. Each side contains four copies of the same x and one added three. Pair those tiles. There are three extra threes in Rule A. Their total is nine. Whatever whole number we pick at the start, Rule A is larger by nine.

1:07 Test the gap at zero and at ten

Check the smallest allowed input. Starting from zero gives twelve and three. Starting from ten gives fifty-two and forty-three. Each difference is nine. In both rows, the starting amount changes the two outputs by the same amount.

1:22 Draw the two add-and-scale rules

The multiplication makes extra copies of the added amount when the addition comes first. Use that observation with two new rules. One adds two before multiplying by six. The other multiplies by six before adding two. Draw the matching pieces and find the gap.
