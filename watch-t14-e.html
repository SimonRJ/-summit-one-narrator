T14.E · Worked example

Turn routes and schedules into networks

Worked example: schedule only the meetings that are required.

1:24 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Six meetings are needed, one for each pair AB, AC, AD, BE, CE and DE. Meetings may share a time slot if nobody has two meetings at once. What is the fewest slots needed?

Check your reasoning

A attends three meetings and needs three separate slots. Three suffice. Put AB with DE, AC with BE, and AD with CE. In each slot all four participants are different.

Read the transcript

0:00 Place five meetings for A, B, C, D and E

Five pupils, A, B, C, D and E, need only these meetings: A with B, A with C, A with D, B with E, and C with E. A pupil can attend at most one meeting in a time slot. What is the smallest number of slots?

0:19 Count the meetings A has to attend

Pupil A is in three meetings. Those three cannot share a slot. So at least three slots are needed.

0:26 Fit the five pairs into three slots

Now try to manage with three. Slot one: A meets B, and C meets E. Slot two: A meets C, and B meets E. Slot three: A meets D.

0:39 Check each pupil appears once per slot they attend

Check each slot. In slot one, the pupils are A, B, C and E, each once. In slot two, the same four, each once. In slot three, only A and D. All five meetings are scheduled with no clashes.

0:54 Check how many pairs fit among five pupils

A second limit agrees. There are five meetings, and a slot holds at most two of them, since five pupils cannot form three pairs. Five meetings need at least three slots. Both limits say three, and three works.

1:09 Add the meeting between D and E

The busiest person gives a limit. Then build a timetable and check every slot. Rebuild this, then decide how many slots are needed if a sixth meeting, D with E, is added.
