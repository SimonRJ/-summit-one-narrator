Read the welcome

Hi Reef. Welcome to Summit One. Get curious, discover clever ideas, and become a more confident problem solver. Your adventure starts with one small step.

You have three exciting destinations: the Australian Mathematics Competition, MAWA's Problem Solving Program, and Rossmoyne's Honours Mathematics programme. Each opens a different door into maths.

Did you know the AMC began in nineteen seventy-eight? Primary divisions joined in two thousand and two. You're joining a long tradition of curious thinkers.

A tricky-looking question can hide a wonderfully simple idea. You'll learn to look beneath the story and notice its mathematical structure.

Watch this. One dot. Add three, then five, then seven. A four-by-four square appears. Suddenly, a sum becomes a picture.

MAWA is the Mathematical Association of Western Australia. Its Problem Solving Program began in nineteen ninety-two. Explaining why something works matters here.

You'll investigate patterns, test small cases, and build convincing explanations. Asking, 'Does this always work?' is where a mathematician's adventure begins.

Rossmoyne's Honours programme explores maths beyond the usual curriculum. Did you know its students form the Fermat Club? They get together to solve problems and have fun.

You'll build number sense, visual thinking, and flexible reasoning. Draw a diagram. Work backwards. Break a big puzzle into smaller pieces.

Each lesson introduces an idea, shows how it works, then helps you try it. Quick choices warm up your thinking before deeper challenges.

Then you'll tackle unfamiliar problems. You'll practise recognising a useful method, even when the numbers and the story have changed.

Getting stuck is part of the work. Try a smaller example. Make a sketch. Check a clue. Every careful attempt gives you something to learn.

Practice papers let you rehearse: read carefully, choose a strategy, manage your time, and check your answers. Afterwards, use the worked solutions to improve.

When exam day arrives, take a breath. Start with what you know. You can move on and return to a tough question. Aim high, one thoughtful step at a time.

You don't need all the answers today, Reef. Bring your curiosity. Build your toolkit. Enjoy those aha moments. Your next discovery starts here.

The stories behind the journey

The background facts were checked against the organisations’ own accounts:

  • Australian Maths Trust: the first AMC — the national competition began in 1978; primary divisions joined in 2002.
  • MAWA: the Problem Solving Program — developed in 1992, with an emphasis on problem solving and presenting explanations.
  • Rossmoyne: Honours Mathematics — mathematics beyond the curriculum and collaborative Fermat Club activities.
