T18.2

Manage a complete attempt and an independent check

Keep a clear trail while solving, and check with a different method.

1:49 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Find the number of border squares of a 7 by 7 grid in two different ways.

Check your reasoning

The inner 5 × 5 square contains every unshaded cell, leaving 7 × 7 − 5 × 5 = 24 on the border. Independently, walk along each side and count its starting corner, stopping before the next corner. Each of the four sides contributes six cells, again giving 24.

Try a variation

How many border squares are there on a 4-by-7 grid? Check your answer a second way.

See the worked answer

Subtract the 2-by-5 inside from the whole grid to get 28 − 10 = 18. Alternatively the top and bottom rows give 14, and the two middle rows add 2 edge squares each, again giving 18.

0:00 Leave a record that another attempt can use

Good working leaves a trail. If you write what you have established, which cases you have finished, and what you will do next, you can pause, return, or find a mistake without starting again.

0:12 Plan a check that can catch an error

Under time pressure, it is easy to repeat work or trust an unchecked answer. Keep a clear trail to show where you stopped, and use an independent check to catch errors.

0:24 Count the border of a five-by-five grid

A five by five grid of small squares has all the squares along its border shaded. How many squares are shaded? Pause and find the answer in one way, then in a second, different way.

0:38 Subtract the unshaded inner square

First method: take away the inside. The whole grid has twenty-five squares. The unshaded inner block is three by three: nine squares. Twenty-five minus nine is sixteen.

0:51 Split the border into four disjoint strips

Second method: split the border into four strips of four squares, each strip starting at a corner and stopping one short of the next. Four times four is sixteen. The two methods agree.

1:04 Correct a count that repeats the corners

A common error is to say four sides of five squares: twenty. That counts each corner twice. Subtract the four corners and you get sixteen again. A second method is valuable precisely because it would have caught that mistake.

1:18 Mark finished cases and a next step

For longer problems, label your cases as you go, and tick each one when it is finished. If you must leave a problem, write one line saying what to do next. Your future self will thank you.

1:30 Check a seven-by-seven border in two ways

Record what you know and what comes next. Tick off finished cases. Then check the answer by a method that could not make the same mistake. Notebook challenge: find the number of border squares of a seven by seven grid in two different ways.
