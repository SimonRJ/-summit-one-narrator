T06.2

Give each component its contribution

Add several measurements so that each unknown appears equally often.

1:53 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Three numbers added in pairs give 17, 20 and 23. Find them.

Check your reasoning

The readings add to 17 + 20 + 23 = 60. Each number appears twice, so the three numbers add to 30. Subtract each pair sum from 30 to get the missing numbers 13, 10 and 7. Their pair sums are 7 + 10 = 17, 7 + 13 = 20 and 10 + 13 = 23.

Read the transcript

0:00 Weigh three boxes two at a time

Sometimes you cannot measure things one at a time. You only know totals of pairs. The useful trick is to add all the measurements and ask how many times each unknown has been counted.

0:12 Find their combined weight first

This replaces trial and error with one clean step. Instead of guessing the three values, you find their total first, and everything else follows by subtraction.

0:22 Record the three pair weights

Three boxes are weighed in pairs. The first and second weigh ten kilograms together. The second and third weigh fourteen. The first and third weigh twelve. How heavy is each box? Pause and try adding all three readings.

0:41 Count each box twice in the total of 36 kg

Add the three readings: ten plus fourteen plus twelve is thirty-six. Look at the table. Each box was on the scales exactly twice. So thirty-six is twice the total weight, and the three boxes weigh eighteen kilograms altogether.

0:56 Recover each box from the other pair

Now subtract. The second and third weigh fourteen, so the first weighs eighteen minus fourteen, which is four. In the same way the second weighs six and the third weighs eight.

1:08 Check all three weights on the scales

Check every reading. Four and six make ten. Six and eight make fourteen. Four and eight make twelve.

1:17 Distinguish triangle corners from edge middles

Watch for unequal counting. In a triangle puzzle where each side has a total, the corner numbers lie on two sides, but the middle numbers lie on only one. Adding the three side totals counts corners twice and middles once. You must keep track of that difference, not ignore it.

1:35 Recover three numbers from their pair sums

Add the measurements. Count how often each unknown appears. Then subtract to separate them. Notebook challenge: three numbers are added in pairs to give seventeen, twenty and twenty-three. What are the three numbers?
