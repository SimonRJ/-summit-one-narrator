Worked example: prove that a number times the next number is always even.

Read the transcript

Prove that any whole number multiplied by the next whole number gives an even result. Three times four is twelve. Six times seven is forty-two. Checking examples is a good start, but the proof must cover numbers we have not checked.

Whole numbers alternate: even, odd, even, odd. So of any two neighbouring numbers, exactly one is even. There are two cases: the first number is even, or the first number is odd.

If the first number is even, it is two lots of something. Multiply it by the next number and the result is still two lots of something. So the product is even.

If the first number is odd, then the next number is even. It is two lots of something, so again the product is two lots of something. The product is even.

A picture confirms it. Arrange the product as a rectangle of dots. It splits into two identical staircases. Two identical halves means the total is even, and each half is one plus two plus three, up to the first number.

The two cases cover every whole number, and each gives an even product. Rebuild the proof, then explain why the product of three whole numbers in a row is always a multiple of three.
