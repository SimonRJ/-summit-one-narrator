T14.2

Use future obligations to reject tempting moves

Reject a move now if it makes the rest of the route impossible.

2:04 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

On a grid of dots with 2 rows and 3 columns, can a path using horizontal and vertical steps visit every dot once, starting at one end of the top row and finishing at the other?

Check your reasoning

No. Those two corners have the same chessboard colour, but a path through 6 dots must start and finish on different colours.

Try a variation

On a 3-by-3 grid of dots, can a path visit every dot exactly once using horizontal or vertical steps if it starts and ends at the middles of two different sides?

See the worked answer

Chessboard colouring gives five dots of the corner colour and four dots of the side-middle colour. A nine-dot path uses its starting colour five times. Starting at a side middle is already impossible.

Read the transcript

0:00 Look ahead before visiting the next dot

Some routes must visit every place exactly once. A move can be perfectly legal now and still ruin the route later, by cutting off a place you still need to visit. Good solvers look ahead at what each move leaves behind.

0:15 Test whether a move cuts off a dot

Without looking ahead you find out about a mistake only at the very end. Checking what remains after each move catches dead ends early and saves most of the work.

0:25 Try a corner-to-centre path through nine dots

A token moves on a three by three grid of dots, stepping to a neighbour up, down, left or right. It must visit every dot exactly once. Starting in a corner, can it finish in the centre? Pause and try to trace it.

0:42 Spiral inward to reach the centre

A spiral works. Go along the top, down the right side, along the bottom, up the left, and step into the centre. Every dot is visited once, and the path ends in the centre.

0:54 Colour the nine dots in alternating colours

Now ask a harder question: can a path start in the middle of a side? Colour the dots like a chessboard. The corners and the centre are dark: five dots. The middles of the sides are light: four dots. Every step changes colour.

1:08 Rule out a start at the middle of a side

A path through nine dots that starts on a light dot would visit light, dark, light, dark, and so on: five light dots and four dark. But there are only four light dots. So no such path exists. One simple count rules out every attempt at once.

1:25 Count the possible endpoints of a route

Here is another look-ahead rule. A dot with only one connection can only be the first or the last dot of the route, because you cannot pass through it. If there are three such dots, no route can visit them all.

1:38 Test two top-row endpoints on a six-dot grid

After each move, check that everything unvisited can still be reached. Dead ends must be endpoints. And colouring can prove impossibility.
