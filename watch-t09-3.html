T09.3

Transform equations without losing conditions

Change an equation only in ways that keep both sides balanced.

1:57 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Every counter weighs the same. Each pouch contains the same number of counters. Three pouches and 4 loose counters balance one pouch and 18 loose counters. The empty pouches have negligible weight. How many counters does each pouch contain?

Check your reasoning

Remove one pouch and four loose counters from both sides. Two pouches then balance 14 counters. Since every counter weighs the same, each pouch contains 7.

Try a variation

Two equal bags and seven loose counters balance one such bag and sixteen counters. How many counters does each bag contain?

See the worked answer

Remove one bag and seven counters from each side. The remaining bag balances 9 counters. Substitution gives 2 × 9 + 7 = 9 + 16 = 25.

Read the transcript

0:00 Undo the same move on both pans

An equation is a balance. Take three from both sides and it stays level. Addition takes us back to the original. Halve both sides and it still balances. Doubling takes us back. A move you can undo keeps exactly the same solutions.

0:16 Preserve every possible solution

Guessing may find an answer. Reversible moves let you solve the equation step by step and confirm you have kept all of its possible answers.

0:25 Balance the pouches and loose counters

Two pouches and five counters balance one pouch and twelve counters. Every pouch holds the same number. How many counters are in a pouch? Pause and think about what you could remove from both sides.

0:41 Remove one pouch from each side

Remove one pouch from each side. The balance holds. Now one pouch and five counters balance twelve counters.

0:48 Clear five loose counters from each pan

Remove five counters from each side. One pouch balances seven counters. Each pouch holds seven.

0:55 Substitute seven into the original balance

Check in the original. Two sevens and five make nineteen. One seven and twelve make nineteen. Both sides agree.

1:04 Clear a half in an equation

With fractions, clear them by multiplying both sides. Half a number plus three is eleven. Take three from both sides: half the number is eight. Double both sides: the number is sixteen.

1:17 Check zero before dividing by the unknown

One caution. Before dividing by an unknown number, check whether it could be zero. If a number times itself equals five times the number and the number is not zero, dividing gives five. Zero also works, because zero times zero equals five times zero. Those two cases cover all possibilities.

1:37 Solve the balance with three pouches

Do the same to both sides. Undo one operation at a time. Check your answer in the original equation. Notebook challenge: three pouches and four counters balance one pouch and eighteen counters. How many counters are in each pouch?
