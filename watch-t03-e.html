Remainders turn long lists into short systems

Worked example: any four whole numbers contain two whose difference is a multiple of 3.

Try a variation

How many distinct whole numbers guarantee that two have a difference divisible by 4? Show why your answer is best possible.

See the worked answer

Division by 4 has four remainder classes. Five numbers force a shared remainder, and subtracting two numbers with the same remainder gives a multiple of 4. Four can fail, as 0, 1, 2 and 3 show.

Read the transcript

0:00 Find a multiple-of-three difference

Choose any four different whole numbers. Prove that two of them always have a difference that is a multiple of three. You can choose very large numbers if you like. How can one argument cover every possible choice?

0:16 Give each number a remainder box

Divide a whole number by three. The remainder can be zero, one or two. A leftover of three would make another complete group. Create one box for each of the three allowable remainders.

Every chosen number belongs to exactly one box. Four numbers and three boxes force at least two numbers into the same box. Those two numbers have the same remainder. We can use that single fact to show their difference is a multiple of three.

0:44 Subtract numbers from the same box

Write the larger number as three a plus r, and the smaller as three b plus the same r. On subtracting, the two r terms cancel. The result is three times the difference between a and b. It is a whole number of groups of three.

0:59 Test four numbers and then three

Try two, six, ten and thirteen. Ten and thirteen both leave remainder one. Their difference is three. Could three numbers guarantee the result? With one, two and three, the remainders all differ. Their differences are one or two. This shows four is the smallest number that guarantees a match.

1:19 Find the guarantee for divisor ten

Choose a box for each remainder and use subtraction once two numbers share a box. Now increase the divisor to ten. How many different whole numbers guarantee that two differ by a multiple of ten? Show both why your answer works and how one fewer choice could fail.
