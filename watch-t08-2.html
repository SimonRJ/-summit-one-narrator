T08.2

Track how one choice changes the next

Follow a probability tree when the first choice changes the second.

1:46 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

A bag contains 3 red counters and 1 blue counter. Draw two at random without putting the first back. What is the probability that both are red?

Check your reasoning

The first counter is red with probability 3/4. If it is red, two red counters remain among three counters. Multiply the two probabilities to get 3/4 × 2/3 = 1/2.

Try a variation

A bag has two blue and three green beads. Two are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability of one bead of each colour?

See the worked answer

Blue then green has probability (2/5)(3/4) = 3/10. Green then blue has probability (3/5)(2/4) = 3/10. The two orders cannot happen together, so the probability is 3/5.

Read the transcript

0:00 Follow a draw without replacement

When you take something out and do not put it back, the situation changes. The second draw is from a different bag. To keep track, follow each possible route step by step.

0:12 Recount the bag after removing one counter

Using the original bag for both draws is a common error. Updating after each step is what makes the answer right.

0:19 Try drawing two reds from four counters

A bag holds two red counters and two blue counters. Draw two counters at random, one after the other, without putting the first back. What is the probability that both are red? Pause and think about the bag after the first draw.

0:37 Set the chance of the first red

On the first draw, two of the four counters are red. The chance of red is two out of four, which is one half.

0:45 Follow the red branch a second time

If the first was red, the bag now has one red and two blue. The chance of red again is one out of three. Along one route, multiply: one half times one third is one sixth. So the probability of two reds is one sixth.

1:01 Check all four colour routes sum to one

Complete the tree. Red then blue is one half times two thirds, one third. Blue then red is also one third. Blue then blue is one sixth. The four routes add to one, which is a good check.

1:15 Combine red–blue and blue–red

What is the chance of one of each colour? That happens on two different routes: red then blue, or blue then red. Different routes are added: one third plus one third is two thirds.

1:27 Draw from three reds and one blue

Update the situation after each step. Multiply along a route. Add separate routes. Notebook challenge: a bag has three red counters and one blue. Two are drawn without replacement. What is the probability both are red?
