Use symmetry without losing or repeating cases

Worked example: why half of all subsets have an even number of members.

1:14 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

From five different objects, how many subsets have an odd number of objects?

Check your reasoning

There are 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32 subsets. Choose one special object. Pair each subset without it with the same subset plus that object. Each pair has one odd-sized subset and one even-sized subset, giving 16 odd-sized subsets.

Try a variation

From six different objects, how many subsets have an even number of members and contain a particular object A?

See the worked answer

Keep A in every subset. The other five objects have 2⁵ = 32 possible selections. Toggling a fixed one of those five pairs selections and changes total parity, so 16 of the resulting subsets have even size.

0:00 Choose groups from A, B and C

From three objects, A, B and C, choose any group, including the empty group with nothing in it. Explain why exactly half of the groups have an even number of members.

0:15 Count all the in-or-out choices

Each object is either in the group or out of it. Two choices, three times over, gives eight groups.

0:23 Add or remove A to make partners

Pair each group with the group you get by changing A: put A in if it is out, and take A out if it is in. The empty group pairs with the group containing only A. The group B, C pairs with the group A, B, C.

0:38 Compare the two sizes in each pair

Changing A changes the size of the group by exactly one. So each pair has one group of even size and one of odd size. Four pairs give four even groups and four odd groups.

0:51 List the four even groups including the empty group

Check by listing. The even groups are the empty group, A with B, A with C, and B with C. That is four, exactly half of eight.

1:00 Count odd groups chosen from five objects

Toggling one member pairs every even group with an odd one. Rebuild this argument, then say how many groups chosen from five objects have an odd number of members.
