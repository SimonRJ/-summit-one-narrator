T17.1

Match the strength of the proof to the claim

Match the kind of evidence to the kind of claim.

1:57 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Is the sum of three odd numbers always odd? Give a reason.

Check your reasoning

Yes: two of the three leftover ones make a pair, leaving exactly one leftover, so the total is odd.

Read the transcript

0:00 Decide what evidence a claim needs

Different claims need different evidence. To show something can happen, one example is enough. To show something always happens or can never happen, your reasons must cover every case. A few examples only settle the cases that you checked.

0:16 Look for a pattern that could break

Patterns can hold for a long time and then break. Knowing what a claim actually requires stops you from trusting a pattern too far, and tells you when your solution is really complete.

0:27 Test a claim about adding two odd numbers

Here is a claim: the sum of two odd numbers is always even. Three and five make eight. Seven and nine make sixteen. Eleven and one make twelve. Is that a proof? Pause and decide.

0:43 Represent any odd number with pairs and a spare

Three examples are not a proof, because there are endlessly many odd numbers. Here is a reason instead. Any odd number is a collection of pairs with one left over.

0:54 Put the two spare counters together

Put two odd numbers together. Their two leftover counters make one more pair. Now everything is in pairs, so the total is even. This works for every pair of odd numbers at once.

1:06 Use nine to test a claim about odd primes

To show a claim is false, one example that breaks it is enough. Someone claims that every odd number bigger than one is prime. Three, five and seven fit. But nine is three times three. One counterexample and the claim is finished.

1:22 Test whether being even guarantees a multiple of six

One more distinction. A condition can be needed without being enough. Every multiple of six is even, so being even is needed. But fourteen is even and is not a multiple of six, so being even is not enough.

1:37 Prove what happens when three odd numbers are added

One example shows something can happen. A reason covering every case shows it always does. One counterexample shows a claim is false. Notebook challenge: is it true that the sum of three odd numbers is always odd? Give a reason, not just examples.
