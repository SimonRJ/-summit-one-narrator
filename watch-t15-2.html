T15.2

Do not divide by a symmetry count blindly

Know when dividing by the number of rotations works, and when it fails.

2:11 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

In how many ways can 4 different people sit round a circular table, if rotations count as the same?

Check your reasoning

24 / 4 = 6.

Read the transcript

Turn a square four times and you might expect four different pictures. But a pattern with its own symmetry repeats sooner. A completely symmetrical pattern looks the same after every turn. So you cannot always divide by four.

Dividing a total by the number of turns is tempting and often wrong. It only works when every pattern has been counted the same number of times.

Each of the four small squares in a two by two tile is black or white. Fixed on the page there are sixteen colourings. How many different tiles are there if the tile is free to turn? Pause, and resist dividing sixteen by four.

Sort the sixteen by how many squares are black. None black: one colouring. One black: four. Two black: six. Three black: four. All black: one.

Now turn them. No black is one tile. The four with one black square are all the same tile. The six with two black squares make two tiles: four are side by side, and two are diagonal. Three black gives one tile, and all black gives one. That is six tiles altogether.

Look at the group sizes: one, four, four, two, four and one. They are not all four. The symmetrical tiles were counted fewer times, which is exactly why sixteen divided by four gives the wrong answer.

When does dividing by the number of turns work? When every turn gives a different position. Seat five different people round a table, keeping clockwise order when you rotate the table. There are one hundred and twenty orders in a line. Each circular seating appears exactly five times as you choose a place to start reading. One hundred and twenty divided by five is twenty-four.

Divide only when every arrangement is repeated the same number of times. Otherwise, sort the arrangements into groups and count the groups. Notebook challenge: in how many ways can four different people sit round a circular table, if turning the whole table does not count as different?
