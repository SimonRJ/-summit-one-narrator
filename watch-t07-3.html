Make equality conditions guide the construction

Use the places where a bound is tight to design the arrangement that reaches it.

1:48 · Full HD

3, for example along a diagonal.

Read the transcript

A proof of a limit is also a set of instructions. To reach the limit exactly, nothing in the proof may be wasted. Looking at where the proof is tight tells you how the best arrangement must be built.

Searching for the best arrangement by trial can take a long time. Letting the proof guide you is faster, and it shows whether the limit can be reached at all.

Counters are placed on a four by four board, at most one per square. Each row may contain at most two counters, and each column at most two. What is the largest number of counters? Pause and find the limit first.

There are four rows, and each holds at most two counters. So there are at most eight counters. That is the limit.

To reach eight, every row must be completely full, with exactly two counters. And the counters must be spread so that no column gets more than two. Putting them all in the first two columns would break the column rule.

So share the columns. Rows one and two use columns one and two. Rows three and four use columns three and four. Every row has two, and every column has two. Eight is reached.

Always check the finished arrangement against every rule, not only the total. An arrangement can have the right number and still break a rule somewhere. Here every row, every column and every square passes.

Prove the limit. Ask what must be full to reach it. Build the arrangement, then check every rule. Notebook challenge: on a three by three board with at most one counter per row and per column, what is the largest number of counters, and where could they go?
