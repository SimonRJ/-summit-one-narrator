T15.1

Define sameness before counting

Decide which arrangements count as "the same" before counting them.

1:54 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Shade three of the four cells of a 2 by 2 square. Patterns that match after a rotation count as the same. How many different patterns are there?

Check your reasoning

Each pattern leaves one corner unshaded. A quarter-turn can move that corner to the next corner, so all four drawings represent the same pattern. There is one.

Read the transcript

Take a square tile with one corner coloured. Turn it, and the coloured corner moves. Is that a new tile, or the same tile seen from another side? The answer depends on the rules of the question. You must decide what counts as the same before you count.

The same problem can have two correct-looking answers, one for objects fixed in place and one for objects that can be turned. Reading which is intended is half the work.

A square is divided into four smaller squares, and exactly one of them is shaded. How many different patterns are there? Pause and decide whether the square is fixed to the page, or free to be turned.

If the square is fixed on the page, like a window in a wall, there are four patterns: top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right.

If the square is a loose tile that can be turned, all four are the same tile. A quarter turn carries each pattern to the next. There is only one pattern.

Now shade two of the four squares. Fixed on the page there are six patterns. Free to turn, they fall into just two kinds: the two shaded squares are side by side, or they are diagonal. Turning can never change one kind into the other.

Turning over is different from turning round. This marked tile cannot match its mirror by turning on the table. Flipping it over changes the order of the marks and makes a match. Some other marks do match their mirrors by turning, so test the moves that the question allows.

Ask whether objects are fixed, can be turned, or can also be flipped. Two arrangements are the same only if an allowed move takes one to the other. Notebook challenge: three of the four small squares are shaded. How many patterns are there if the square is free to turn?
