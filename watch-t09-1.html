T09.1

Keep the whole visible

Name the whole before using any fraction or percentage.

1:54 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

A full tank: one fifth is used, then one quarter of the remainder. What fraction is left?

Check your reasoning

4/5 remains, then 3/4 of that: 3/5 of the tank.

Try a variation

A bottle is three quarters full. You drink one third of the water in it. What fraction of the bottle’s full capacity remains?

See the worked answer

You keep two thirds of the water that was present. Multiplying those two thirds by the original three quarters gives (2/3)(3/4) = 1/2 of the bottle’s capacity.

Read the transcript

0:00 Find a half before and after cutting the bar

A fraction always means a fraction of something. Half of a whole bar is six squares. Half of what is left is only three squares. Same fraction, different whole, different amount. Before using any fraction, say what it is a fraction of.

0:17 Change the whole between steps

This matters most when things change in stages. A second fraction or percentage usually applies to the new amount, not the original. Forgetting that is the classic trap.

0:27 Name the whole for each person’s marbles

A jar holds forty marbles. Ali takes one quarter of them. Then Bea takes one third of what remains. How many marbles are left? Pause and name the whole for each fraction.

0:42 Remove a quarter of the original forty

Ali's quarter is a quarter of forty, which is ten. Thirty marbles remain.

0:48 Take a third of the remaining thirty

Bea's third is a third of thirty, not of forty. That is ten more. Twenty marbles are left. A third of the original forty would split a marble, a clue that we have used the wrong whole.

1:01 Model two thirds of three quarters

A grid shows a fraction of a fraction. Shade three of four columns: three quarters. Then take two of the three rows of that part: two thirds of three quarters. The overlap is six cells out of twelve, which is one half. To find a fraction of a fraction, multiply.

1:18 Raise and lower a price by ten percent

Now the price trap. A one hundred dollar item goes up by ten percent, to one hundred and ten. Then it goes down by ten percent. Ten percent of one hundred and ten is eleven, so the price becomes ninety-nine. The two changes do not cancel, because they are percentages of different wholes.

1:36 Track the remaining water in a tank

Always ask: fraction of what? Update the whole after each step. Notebook challenge: a tank is full. One fifth is used on Monday, and one quarter of the remainder on Tuesday. What fraction of the tank is left?
