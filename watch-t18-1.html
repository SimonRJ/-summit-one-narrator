T18.1

Start with the structure that limits the choices

Begin a problem with its tightest restriction.

1:57 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

A number between 50 and 100 is an odd multiple of 13. What could it be?

Check your reasoning

Multiples of 13 in range: 52, 65, 78, 91. The odd ones are 65 and 91.

Read the transcript

0:00 Find the condition that narrows the search

Most problems have several conditions, and they are not equally powerful. One of them usually cuts the possibilities down to a handful. Start with that one, and the others become quick checks.

0:13 Compare the sizes of possible starting lists

Starting with a weak condition leaves you searching through hundreds of cases. Starting with the strongest can leave three or four. Choosing where to begin is a strategy decision worth ten seconds of thought.

0:25 Find a two-digit square with digit sum ten

A two-digit number is a perfect square, and its digits add to ten. What is it? Pause and decide which clue to use first.

0:36 Compare the digit-sum list with the six squares

The digit-sum clue allows nine numbers. The square clue allows only six: sixteen, twenty-five, thirty-six, forty-nine, sixty-four and eighty-one. Start with the squares.

0:50 Test the digit sums of the six candidates

Add the digits of each: seven, seven, nine, thirteen, ten, nine. Only sixty-four has digits adding to ten. The answer is sixty-four.

1:01 Use area to list possible rectangle sides

A product is another strong restriction. A rectangle has whole-number sides, area twenty-four and perimeter twenty. The area allows only four shapes: one by twenty-four, two by twelve, three by eight, and four by six. Their perimeters are fifty, twenty-eight, twenty-two and twenty. It is four by six.

1:24 Match factors, totals and cycles to question clues

Certain features point to certain tools. A product suggests factors. A fixed total suggests parts. A repeating process suggests a cycle. An order suggests a line. These are suggestions to try, not guarantees.

1:38 Find the odd multiples of thirteen in the interval

List the conditions. Start with the one that leaves the fewest possibilities. Use the rest as checks. Notebook challenge: a number between fifty and one hundred is a multiple of thirteen and is odd. What could it be?
