T05.1

Decide whether order belongs to the outcome

Decide whether swapping two chosen objects makes a new outcome.

1:57 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Six players are available. How many ways can you choose a captain and a different vice-captain? How many ways can you choose two of the players to carry equipment together?

Check your reasoning

For the two roles there are 6 × 5 = 30 choices. For the equipment team, choosing A then B gives the same team as choosing B then A. Each team has been counted twice, so there are 15 teams.

Try a variation

Four pupils are available. How many two-person teams can they form, and how many choices are there for a speaker and a different recorder?

See the worked answer

The distinct roles give 4 × 3 = 12 assignments. For a team, reversing the two names repeats exactly the same team, so there are 12 ÷ 2 = 6 teams.

Read the transcript

0:00 Swap two runners on a podium and on a team

Before counting anything, ask one question: does order matter? Choosing a winner and a runner-up is different from choosing a team of two. On the podium, swapping the two people changes the result. In a team, swapping them changes nothing.

0:16 Match two finish tickets to one team

We divide by two because each team was counted in two orders. The number you divide by is the number of ways to arrange the chosen objects.

Why two orders? In the opening example, two distinct players can exchange places. There are two orders for that pair. For three distinct players there are 3 × 2 × 1 = 6 orders, so an unordered group of three needs a different divisor.

0:27 Compare both selections from Ava, Ben and Cal

Three runners, Ava, Ben and Cal, are in a race. In how many ways can first and second place be filled? And how many different pairs of them could form a team? Pause and try both.

0:42 Assign first place and second place

For the podium, there are three choices for first place, then two remaining choices for second. Three times two is six ordered results.

0:51 Merge two orders that pick the same team

For a team, Ava then Ben is the same team as Ben then Ava. The six ordered results merge in pairs, leaving three teams.

1:04 Count the six orders of each group of three

Now choose three people from five. In order, that is five times four times three, which is sixty. As an unordered group, each set of three people can be arranged in six orders, so sixty divided by six gives ten groups.

1:24 Assign captain, scorer and timekeeper

Read the wording carefully. If the three chosen people get different jobs, such as captain, scorer and timekeeper, then order matters again and the answer is sixty.

1:35 Choose captains or equipment carriers

If positions or roles differ, count in order. If only membership matters, divide by the number of arrangements of the chosen group. Notebook challenge: from six players, how many ways are there to choose a captain and a vice-captain? And how many ways to choose two players to carry the equipment?
