# Source audit: APSMO and other problem-solving books
**Review date:** 13 November 2026

## Recovered primary source
The previously uploaded APSMO/Pauline Kohlhoff *Building Confidence in Maths Problem Solving* Volume 1 is preserved as a substantial series of photographed double-page spreads in the user's AMC Training Library and prior Library image collection. The photo sequence includes IMG_7170–IMG_7272 (with some duplicate photos). The original images are **not** to be copied into public GitHub Pages.

Reviewed photographed spreads:
- IMG_7170, pp.4–5: paired difference and bar-model arguments; a routine early teaching prerequisite (Q1–10 baseline), not sufficiently challenging on its own for AMC Q21–30.
- IMG_7190, pp.44–45: mixed strategy practice: change of group size, height-order comparisons, matchstick recurrences, weekdays, digit symbols, option counting, constraint grids and overlap geometry. Use as *curriculum coverage cues*, not as questions to duplicate.
- IMG_7200, pp.64–65: three-stack working-backwards model, organised digit counting with repeated digits; valuable for Q11–20 and deeper transfer with different mathematical structures.
- IMG_7215, pp.94–95: equally spaced points, smaller-related-problem reasoning, magic square elimination; build deeper proofs of why a method works.
- IMG_7225, pp.114–115: two-step rates/averages, permutations, maximum shape perimeter, relative order constraints, surface area of composite solids. Strong source for skill distribution and teaching representations.
- IMG_7241, pp.146–147: linear combinations and set-of-possible-totals from a fixed number of choices; good structure for Q16–25, provided new problems use genuinely different insight and constraints.
- IMG_7249, pp.162–163: alphametics/digit-place equations and organised counting of all sub-squares; use for method explanations and originality audit.
- IMG_7260, pp.184–185: multiplicative sharing, modular constraints, cube nets, divisor/remainder reasoning; use to sharpen number/spatial readiness.
- IMG_7272, pp.208–209: serpentine tables/counting patterns and square dissection with several independent proofs; appropriate enrichment for later difficulty.

## Topic allocation recommended for NEW authored exams
**Q1–10 (3 marks):** no trick dependence; multi-representation fluency, place value, basic spatial sense, operations, time and unit conversions, fractions, introductory tables.
**Q11–20 (4 marks):** choice of representation, two constraints, visual counting, parity/remainders in short contexts, scale and ratios, non-obvious perimeter/area, inference from small cases.
**Q21–25 (5 marks):** require a precise model, efficient elimination, clever equivalence, mixed domains and plausible misleading choices. Use diagrams when needed.
**Q26–30 (6–10 marks):** increasingly difficult structural problems: invariants, restricted construction, exact enumeration, spatial unfolding, digit/algebra insight, geometric dissection. Do not copy source diagrams, wording, examples or solution chains.

## Quality and permission constraints
Each new exam problem must be separately authored, independently solved and verified. Reject clones that merely change objects, variables or values of previous questions. Store source inspirations as private provenance metadata in the protected database, not bundled with the public web app. Student-facing app should not identify copyrighted source pages in a way that reproduces their problems.
