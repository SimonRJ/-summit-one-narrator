# Five quick decisions in Discover

Added 12 November 2026.

Every teaching lesson has five A/B cards after the small worked examples and before its existing guided question. MAWA investigations have five cards following their warm-up. Timed mocks and genuine papers retain their exam flow.

The cards practise mathematical decisions that are used in the next full problems: reading the requested quantity, identifying a useful relationship, selecting a first move, using that move on a small example, and rejecting a plausible mistake. Examples include remainder versus space left in a group, objects versus intervals, weighted averages, required versus forbidden routes, and a necessary condition versus a proof that a construction works.

There are explicit decks for all 52 techniques and all 20 investigation topics, covering 283 lessons and 53 investigation visits. Investigation decks reuse a core technique only where it fits the investigation; specialised topics have their own decision cards. Repeated visits revisit the principles, with varied values where appropriate and balanced, shuffled answer positions.

Each card gives an immediate reason after the first choice. Learners may continue after an incorrect choice once they have read the explanation. The recap highlights missed distinctions. First choices and the current card are saved in the existing session draft, including recovery from a failed cloud save. Cards use buttons, visible focus, A/B keys while focus is within the quiz, and a one-column mobile layout.

The warm-up aims to take about two or three minutes, with no countdown or speed reward. Its score is recognition practice, never independent mastery or exam credit. The existing original problems, written reasoning, transfer work and delayed recall remain the evidence of application. In particular, an A/B choice can be guessed; it is not evidence that a pupil can solve the full problem independently.

Checks cover all 336 teaching/investigation sessions, balanced and deterministic answer positions, invalid-topic rejection, representative arithmetic, preserved originals, saving/resuming, correction feedback, keyboard/touch input, mobile layout and exam regression. New prompts and choices were screened against the installed 2007–2025/archive text corpus; no exact/contained text match was found. This is not a claim that textual screening proves absence of all possible semantic similarities.
