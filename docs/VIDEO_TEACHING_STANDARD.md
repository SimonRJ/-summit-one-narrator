# Summit One — Teaching video structure

This guide applies to new and revised narrated concept or worked-example videos. The benchmark is original mathematical animation with the clarity and continuous visual reasoning associated with premium explanatory mathematics videos. Do not copy any creator's assets, scripts, or signature sequences.

## The default lesson arc

1. **Introduce the insight.** Before the puzzle, tell the learner what they will learn, define unfamiliar vocabulary in plain language, and show a simple moving visual to anchor the concept.
2. **Explain why it matters.** Connect the insight to a common AMC trap: excessive casework, calculations, misreading a condition, or a proof that is missing. Say specifically what the technique saves or makes possible.
3. **Pose an accessible puzzle.** Read the problem clearly, define what actions are legal, and visually show the initial state and goal. Allow a short moment to think. Avoid revealing the answer in the first frame.
4. **Develop and prove the idea.** Animate one meaningful step at a time. Make the invariant, symmetry, pattern, equation, or insight emerge *from the diagram*. Include the why, not just an answer.
5. **Work through at least two examples** when appropriate: a direct application and one variation that reveals a common misconception or a changed condition. Explain the difference between a necessary test and a sufficient construction whenever relevant.
6. **Conclude and transfer.** Recap the reusable method in simple terms, distinguish what was proved from what was only observed, and finish with a short independent notebook exercise or challenge.

**Do not begin immediately with a puzzle** for a new concept. Allocate meaningful narrated screen time to the first two stages; for a 3–5 minute concept video, usually around 35–55 seconds combined. Adapt to topic complexity rather than enforcing a rigid duration.

## Production requirements

- Animations should show transformation, correspondence, cancellation, re-grouping, comparison or movement; do not merely fade in slides of text.
- Keep the 16:9 1280×720 layout and reserve a safe caption area, clear of mathematical visuals.
- Record natural connected narration with variable punctuation-aware pauses; avoid robotic fixed-gap phrasing and never use the retired George voice.
- Timings come from the final generated audio, not estimated reading speeds; do not time-stretch speech artificially.
- Keep exactly **one** visible caption layer. If captions are burned in, do not enable a second in-player text track by default. Smoothly emphasise only the current spoken word using the audio alignment.
- Verify mathematical correctness (including edge cases), audio clarity, speech/caption alignment, legibility on a phone, and the actual published HTTPS link.
- The original T02.1 edit introduced three opening scenes before its existing puzzle and extended the visual structure from 18 to 21 scenes. Only adopt the visual standard course-wide after student review.
