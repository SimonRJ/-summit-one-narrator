## Public video survey · November 19

The inventory covers 113 published lesson introductions and 72 teaching films. For all 185 source movies, the downloaded public bytes matched the catalogue SHA-256 or the repository media blob. Both streams decoded through the end. The survey extracted 3,678 keyframes and indexed three representative frames for each film. A second pass measured picture changes four times per second across the complete movies, excluding the header and subtitles. This produced 117,162 samples. The longest quiet intervals identify places to inspect. They do not measure mathematical accuracy or judge the value of a pause.

The row for each film records its source hash, sample times, current published edition and the elapsed playback time immediately after a real chapter jump in each phone-sized browser. Blank playback cells identify outstanding checks. The original source and current edition can differ where a correction was published after the survey began. WebKit and Chrome used 390 × 844 and 844 × 390 viewports. For the editions in this revision, 740 of 740 film, browser and orientation combinations have recorded a successful start, seek and resume. The chapter controls for four teaching films were repaired after landscape WebKit exposed an unreliable seek. T02.1 uses its native seek control. The visual and motion survey does not establish a complete editorial watch and listen of every film.

Among 88 introductions whose original recording script matched the unillustrated production source exactly, 83 reused the same diagram specification between the basic question and its explanation. The same pattern occurred in 77 medium examples and 65 challenges. Those counts compare geometry and objects in the source specifications. They do not count reworked editions or infer that every repetition was inappropriate. The recurring failure was a picture that stayed fixed when the narration described a mathematical change.

## Required evidence before marking a film complete

1. Identify the actual film at its lesson route and its matching transcript and audio; do not substitute a similarly named script.
2. Review the whole film, including intermediate motion, and check that every illustrated quantity, colour, dimension, shape, condition, calculation and caption agrees with the spoken example.
3. Independently calculate the examples, including boundary cases. Ensure motion develops the mathematical idea, not merely a sequence of text cards.
4. Check caption readability and timing, one caption layer, and phone-sized playback. Preserve the audio where approved and correct.
5. Publish only the individually reviewed replacement, confirm its actual HTTPS playback, and record what was and was not verified. Do not call a file complete solely because it encodes, uploads, or plays.
