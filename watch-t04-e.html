T04.E · Worked example

Use prime factors as the building blocks

Worked example: the smallest multiplier that turns 90 into a perfect square.

1:43 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Find the smallest positive whole number that multiplies 60 to make a perfect square.

Check your reasoning

In 60 = 2² × 3 × 5, the two twos already form a pair. Any multiplier that makes a square must supply at least one 3 and one 5, so it is a multiple of 15. The first positive choice works because 60 × 15 = 900 = 30².

Try a variation

What is the smallest positive whole number that multiplies 54 to make a perfect square?

See the worked answer

Since 54 = 2 × 3³, both prime exponents are odd. Supply one more 2 and one more 3. The smallest multiplier is 6, and 54 × 6 = 324 = 18².

Read the transcript

0:00 Turn 90 into a square

What is the smallest positive whole number that you can multiply ninety by to make a perfect square? Try to think of ninety as a collection of prime pieces. A square will let those pieces split into two identical groups.

0:18 Find the two primes without partners

Ninety factorises into two times three times three times five. Each prime needs an even number of copies in a square. The threes already make a pair. The two and the five each appear once. Put a space for a partner beside each of them.

0:33 Supply one more two and one more five

Supply one more two and one more five. Those are the missing tiles. Their product is ten, so multiplying ninety by ten adds just the two partners we need. Now each prime has an even count.

0:46 Split 900 into matching groups

Ninety times ten is nine hundred. Split its tiles into two identical groups, each with a two, a three and a five. Each group multiplies to thirty. Thirty times thirty is nine hundred, so the new number is a square.

1:02 Show that every valid multiplier is a multiple of ten

Could a smaller positive multiplier work? Any multiplier that works must supply a two and a five, because those counts start odd. A number divisible by the two distinct primes two and five is a multiple of ten. Ten is the first positive multiple and we have shown it works.

1:20 Find the least multiplier for 60

For a square, pair every prime. Choose one partner for each prime whose count is odd. Try the same proof with sixty. Find the smallest positive whole number to multiply sixty by to make a square, and check your answer by splitting the new prime tiles into two equal groups.
