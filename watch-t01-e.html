T01.E · Worked example

Read the question into a useful model

Find a length or an elapsed time by measuring the interval between two positions.

2:21 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

A camera starts filming at 10:47 am and stops at 12:06 pm on the same day. How many minutes does it record?

Check your reasoning

13 minutes to 11:00 am, 60 to noon, 6 more to 12:06 pm. The total is 13 + 60 + 6 = 79 minutes.

Read the transcript

0:00 Locate the two ends of the ribbon

A ribbon lies along a centimetre ruler. Its left edge lines up with two, and its right edge lines up with nine. How long is the ribbon? Look at where it begins and ends, then try to explain your first move.

The mark labelled two is two centimetres from zero. The mark labelled nine is nine centimetres from zero. The question asks for the distance from the first position to the second. Trace that part of the ruler with your finger.

0:32 Count centimetre gaps from 2 to 9

Move from two to three. That is the first centimetre. From three to four is the second. Every small step adds one centimetre. Keep going until you reach nine. There are seven gaps of one centimetre, so the ribbon is seven centimetres long.

0:48 Subtract the starting mark

The nine centimetres from zero to the end includes two centimetres before the ribbon begins. Remove those two centimetres. Nine minus two leaves seven. You can count eight marked points across the ribbon because both ends have a mark. Each centimetre lives in the space between two marks.

1:07 Slide both ends back to zero and seven

Slide the ribbon two centimetres to the left. Watch both ends move the same distance. It starts at zero and reaches seven. The two endpoints changed together, and the span stayed seven centimetres. That is another way to check our answer.

1:22 Time a film from 7:15 to 9:05

The same idea works with time. A film starts at seven fifteen in the evening and ends at nine oh five. How many minutes pass? Draw a timeline. Put seven fifteen on the left. Mark eight o'clock and nine o'clock along the way, and stop at nine oh five.

From seven fifteen to eight o'clock is forty-five minutes. The next hour gives sixty minutes. From nine o'clock to the end is five more. Add forty-five, sixty and five. The total is one hundred and ten minutes. That is one hour and fifty minutes.

1:59 Measure a recording across noon

Label the two positions. Find the length, or the time, between them. Use equal steps and check the units. Now try your own timeline. A camera starts filming at ten forty-seven in the morning and stops at twelve oh six that day. How many minutes does it record?
