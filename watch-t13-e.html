Worked example: two cuts of the same size with different effects on perimeter.

1:18 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

From a 6 by 4 rectangle, a 2 by 1 rectangle is cut from the middle of an edge, its long side along the edge. New perimeter?

Check your reasoning

Remove 2, add 1 + 2 + 1 = 4: 20 + 2 = 22 cm.

Read the transcript

Start with two rectangles, each six centimetres by four. Cut a one centimetre square from a corner of the first, and a one centimetre square from the middle of an edge of the second. What are the two new perimeters?

Each original rectangle has perimeter six plus four plus six plus four: twenty centimetres.

The corner cut removes two one-centimetre pieces of the old boundary, and exposes two new one-centimetre pieces. The change is zero. The perimeter is still twenty centimetres.

The cut in the middle of an edge removes only one piece of old boundary, but exposes three new pieces. The change is plus two. The perimeter is twenty-two centimetres.

Both shapes have lost the same area. Each has area twenty-three square centimetres. Yet their perimeters differ. The area removed does not decide the change in perimeter; the position of the cut does.

New perimeter equals old perimeter, minus the boundary removed, plus the boundary created. Rebuild this, then find the perimeter when a two by one rectangle is cut from the middle of an edge, with its long side along the edge.
