The learning sequence

  1. Recognise: what is being asked, which clues matter, and which tool could fit?
  2. Understand: follow a small worked example and explain its first decision.
  3. Decide quickly: five A/B decisions, with reasons and precise wording.
  4. Build independence: try a supported problem, then solve an original problem with less help.
  5. Transfer: choose a method on a mixed problem without a technique label or a worked diagram giving away the approach.
  6. Repair and return: identify the cause of a mistake and revisit a changed example after more completed sessions.

Keep some papers for a fresh check

Use original practice papers to rehearse timing. Save several authentic papers for later, supervised 60-minute checks. Repeating a mock is useful practice; its first score and later scores answer different questions about learning.

A full score is the goal. Neither completing lessons nor memorising a paper guarantees an exam result.
