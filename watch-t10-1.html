T10.1

Build a recurrence from a reversible step

Count large cases by building them from smaller cases.

1:45 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

How many ways to tile a strip of length 7 with tiles of length 1 and 2?

Check your reasoning

1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21: there are 21.

Try a variation

A strip of length 5 is tiled with pieces of length 1 and 2. How many tilings end with a length-2 piece?

See the worked answer

Remove the final length-2 piece. The remaining length 3 has exactly three tilings, namely 1+1+1, 1+2 and 2+1. Appending a 2 returns one valid length-5 tiling for each, giving 3.

Read the transcript

0:00 Build a large count from smaller cases

Some counting problems are too big to list, but they grow in a regular way. If you can explain how each case is built from smaller ones, you can climb to any size. That building rule is called a recurrence.

0:14 Tile ten cells without listing 89 patterns

A strip of ten cells can be tiled in eighty-nine ways. Nobody wants to list those. A recurrence gets the answer with nine small additions.

0:23 Predict the count for five cells

A strip is covered using tiles of length one and tiles of length two. A strip of length one can be tiled in one way, length two in two ways, length three in three ways, length four in five ways. How many ways are there for length five? Pause and look at how a tiling ends.

0:44 Sort tilings by their last tile

Look at the last tile. Either it is a single, and the rest is a tiling of length four. Or it is a double, and the rest is a tiling of length three. Every tiling ends in exactly one of these two ways.

0:57 Add the length-four and length-three counts

So the number for length five is the number for length four plus the number for length three. Five plus three is eight.

1:05 Continue the tiling counts to ten cells

Keep going. Eight plus five is thirteen. Then twenty-one, thirty-four, fifty-five, eighty-nine. Each number is the sum of the two before it.

1:15 Match the last tile to the last stair step

The same rule appears in disguise. Climbing stairs one or two steps at a time is the same problem: the last move is a single step or a double. Six stairs can be climbed in thirteen ways.

1:28 Count the tilings of seven cells

Sort the possibilities by their last move. Each group matches a smaller case. Add the groups. Notebook challenge: how many ways are there to tile a strip of length seven with singles and doubles?
