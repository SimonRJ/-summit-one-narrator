T01.1

Give every quantity a job

Attach a meaning and a unit to every quantity before calculating.

2:40 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

A shelf holds 6 boxes, each with 8 pencils. Five pencils are broken. How many unbroken pencils are there?

Check your reasoning

6 × 8 = 48 pencils and 48 − 5 = 43 unbroken pencils.

Read the transcript

When a maths problem gives you three numbers, each number can do a different job. One might count packets. Another might tell you how many counters fit in each packet. The third might count counters on their own. Give every number its job before you calculate.

Start with the question: how many counters altogether? That tells us the unit of our answer. Four plus three plus two gives nine, but we would be adding a packet count to two different descriptions of counters. Give each quantity its label and ask what it would take to get everything into counters.

Mia has four packets of counters. Each packet contains three counters, and Mia has two loose counters as well. How many counters does she have altogether? Before you work it out, point to what the four, the three and the two each describe.

Four tells us how many groups there are. Three tells us the size of every group. Open the first packet and you can see three counters. Open the others and the same group repeats. Four groups of three give twelve counters. Multiplication gives the counter total for the packets.

The two loose counters are already in the unit we want. Slide them into the collection. Now we are adding twelve counters and two counters, and the total is fourteen counters. Check it in the picture: four rows of three, and two beside them.

The same idea works with measurements. One ribbon is two metres long, and another is thirty centimetres long. How much ribbon is there altogether? Two metres is two hundred centimetres. Both lengths are now in centimetres, so together they measure two hundred and thirty centimetres.

A number can have a different job even when it uses the same digits. Think of a book with pages numbered ninety-five through one hundred and five. In just that range, how many times is the digit nine written? Look across the page numbers and find each printed nine.

Five page numbers contain a nine: ninety-five, ninety-six, ninety-seven, ninety-eight and ninety-nine. The last page number on that list contains two nines. Its first nine counts once and its second nine counts once. There are six written nines in all.

Read the final question. Label each number with what it counts or measures. Bring quantities into a common unit when you need to add them. Here is your notebook challenge. Six boxes hold eight pencils each. Five pencils are broken. How many unbroken pencils are there? Label the quantities and show your calculation.
