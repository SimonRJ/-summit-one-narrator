T16.3

Recover totals and interpret averages

Turn an average back into a total before doing anything else.

1:57 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Read the transcript

The mean is what everyone would have if the total were shared equally. So a mean always hides a total: the mean multiplied by the number of items. Whenever a question mentions an average, recover the total first.

When groups have different sizes, their averages carry different weight. Recover the total in each group before combining them. Then each individual item makes the right contribution.

Four scores have a mean of seven. A fifth score is added and the mean becomes eight. What is the fifth score? Pause and find the totals.

Four scores with mean seven total twenty-eight. Five scores with mean eight total forty.

The fifth score supplies the difference: forty minus twenty-eight is twelve. To average eight, the original four scores would need thirty-two points. They have twenty-eight, so they are four points short. The fifth score supplies its own eight points and those four extra points.

Combining groups works the same way. Two people average six and three people average ten. The totals are twelve and thirty, forty-two for five people. The combined mean is eight point four, not eight. It leans towards the larger group.

The mean is not the only average. The median is the middle value when the list is in order. For two, three, five, nine and thirty-one, the median is five, while the mean is ten. One very large value pulls the mean up but leaves the median alone.

The total is the mean times the number of items. Do your reasoning with totals. And the median is the middle value. Notebook challenge: the mean of six numbers is nine. One number is removed and the mean of the rest is eight. Which number was removed?
