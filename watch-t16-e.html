Keep ratios, rates and averages precise

Worked example: why you cannot average two averages.

1:15 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Four learners score 5 each and one learner scores 10. What is the mean?

Check your reasoning

The four scores of 5 contribute 20 points. The other score brings the total to 30. Sharing those 30 points across all five learners gives a mean of 6 points.

Try a variation

Three pupils average 12 points. Two others average 7 points. What is the average for all five?

See the worked answer

The group totals are 3 × 12 = 36 and 2 × 7 = 14. The total of 50 points shared across five pupils gives a mean of 10.

Read the transcript

0:00 Combine the scores of two groups of learners

Two learners each score seven points, and three learners each score nine points. What is the mean score for all five learners?

0:11 Total the sevens and the nines separately

Find the totals. The first group scores two times seven: fourteen points. The second group scores three times nine: twenty-seven points.

0:21 Share 41 points equally among five learners

Together that is forty-one points for five learners. Forty-one divided by five is eight point two. The mean score is eight point two.

0:31 Explain why the mean moves towards nine

Why is the mean above eight, halfway between seven and nine? Three learners score nine and two score seven, so the balance point is pulled towards nine. If you add one extra score of nine to four scores averaging eight, the mean moves one fifth of the way from eight to nine.

0:48 Balance the groups with equal numbers of learners

If the groups were the same size, two learners at seven and two at nine, the mean would be exactly eight. The two group means now carry equal weight, so their simple average works.

1:00 Find the mean of four fives and one ten

Combine the totals, then divide by the total number of people. Rebuild this, then find the mean score when four learners score five each and one learner scores ten.
