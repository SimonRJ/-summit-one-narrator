Let the experiment determine the probability

Worked example: "at least one head" shrinks the comparison group.

1:10 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

Two fair coins are tossed independently and show different faces. What is the probability the first is heads?

Check your reasoning

Survivors HT, TH; one of two: 1/2.

Try a variation

Two fair six-sided dice are rolled independently. You are told their sum is 5. What is the probability that the first die shows 1?

See the worked answer

The four surviving ordered outcomes are (1,4), (2,3), (3,2) and (4,1). Each had the same original probability. Only (1,4) starts with 1, so the answer is 1/4.

Read the transcript

0:00 Compare two heads with at least one head

Two fair coins are tossed independently. You know that at least one of them shows heads. What is the probability that both show heads?

0:12 Label the two coins and list four results

Name the coins first and second. There are four equally likely outcomes: head head, head tail, tail head, tail tail.

0:20 Remove the outcome with two tails

"At least one head" rules out tail tail. Three outcomes survive: head head, head tail and tail head. They are still equally likely.

0:30 Find two heads among the surviving results

Only one of the three survivors has two heads. The probability is one out of three.

0:36 Change the clue to a head on the first coin

Compare a slightly different piece of information. Suppose you are told the first coin shows heads. Now only head head and head tail survive, and the probability of two heads is one half. The exact wording of the information matters.

0:51 Condition on the coins showing different faces

Information removes outcomes, and the survivors become the new total. Rebuild this example, then try: two coins are tossed and you are told they do not show the same face. What is the probability the first coin is heads?
