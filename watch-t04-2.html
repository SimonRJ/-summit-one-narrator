T04.2

Count divisors by making independent choices

Count the factors of a number by choosing how many of each prime to use.

2:05 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

How many positive factors does 200 have?

Check your reasoning

Since 200 = 2³ × 5², a factor uses 0, 1, 2 or 3 twos and 0, 1 or 2 fives. That gives 4 × 3 = 12 positive factors, including 1 when both counts are zero. Each pair of exponents gives a different prime factorisation.

Try a variation

How many positive divisors of 72 are odd?

See the worked answer

Write 72 = 2³ × 3². An odd divisor uses no factor of 2. Its power of 3 can be 0, 1 or 2, giving the three divisors 1, 3 and 9.

Read the transcript

0:00 Pair the positive factors of twelve

Twelve has six positive factors: one, two, three, four, six and twelve. We can list those by finding the products that make twelve. For a larger number, it is easier to count choices from its prime factorisation.

Three hundred and sixty factorises into two cubed, three squared and five. Its full factor list has twenty-four entries. We can account for them through three small counts, one for each prime. Let's see how this works with twelve first.

0:31 Choose the twos and threes in a factor

Twelve is two times two times three. A factor of twelve is made from some of those prime tiles. Think about how many twos you can use. Then think about your choices for the three. How many different products are possible?

0:47 Find 1 and 12 in the choices grid

Use zero, one or two twos. That gives three choices. For each of them, either include the three or leave it out. That gives two choices. Three times two makes six products. Taking no tiles gives one. Taking all the tiles gives twelve. Every cell makes a different factor.

1:07 Extend the count from 72 to 360

Seventy-two is two cubed times three squared. Choose zero, one, two or three twos. For each choice there are three possibilities for the threes. Four times three gives twelve factors. For three hundred and sixty, the extra choice about the five doubles that count to twenty-four.

1:25 Find the unpaired factor of 36

For thirty-six, the factor pairs are one and thirty-six, two and eighteen, three and twelve, and four and nine. Six pairs with itself, so it appears just once in the list. A factor pairs with itself exactly when its square is the number. That is why square numbers have an odd count of positive factors.

1:45 Count the factors of 200

For each prime, include the choice to use zero copies. Add one to the prime's exponent, and multiply the resulting counts. In your notebook, find the number of positive factors of two hundred, then explain why your method counts each factor once.
