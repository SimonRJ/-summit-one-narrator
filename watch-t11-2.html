T11.2

Use information that survives rearrangement

Use digit sums to find remainders on division by 9 and by 3.

2:00 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

One digit is missing from the four-digit number 73□4. Which digit makes the number divisible by 9?

Check your reasoning

The known digits add to 14. The digit sum can range from 14 to 23, which contains only one multiple of 9. It must be 18, so the missing digit is 4.

Try a variation

Rearrange the digits 1, 4 and 7. Can any resulting number be divisible by 9? Can any be divisible by 3?

See the worked answer

Every ordering has digit sum 12. That sum is divisible by 3 and not by 9, so every resulting number is divisible by 3 and none is divisible by 9.

0:00 Compare each place value with a multiple of 9

Ten is one more than nine. A hundred is one more than ninety-nine. A thousand is one more than nine hundred and ninety-nine. Every place value is one more than a multiple of nine. That is why digit sums work.

0:14 Use digit sums after rearranging digits

This gives a test for multiples of nine, and of three, that takes seconds, even for enormous numbers. It also survives when digits are rearranged.

0:24 Test 4725 for divisibility by 9

Is four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five a multiple of nine? Pause and add its digits.

0:33 Remove groups of nine from each place

Each thousand leaves one over after removing nines. So four thousands leave four. Seven hundreds leave seven. Two tens leave two. The five units stay. What is left over is exactly the digit sum.

0:47 Explain why a digit sum of 18 works

Four plus seven plus two plus five is eighteen. Eighteen is a multiple of nine, so the number is too. In general, a number and its digit sum leave the same remainder when divided by nine.

1:00 Find the remainder when 583 is divided by 9

The digit sum lets us find the remainder, too. Five hundred and eighty-three has digit sum sixteen. Remove as many complete groups of nine from sixteen as you can. Sixteen is seven more than nine. So five hundred and eighty-three leaves remainder seven when divided by nine.

1:18 Rearrange 1, 2 and 6 and test for 3

Rearranging digits does not change their sum. So every arrangement of the digits one, two and six is a multiple of nine. And since nine is a multiple of three, the same digit-sum test works for three.

1:31 Fill the missing digit between 5 and 8

It solves missing digits too. Five, blank, eight is a multiple of nine. The known digits add to thirteen, so the blank must be five, making eighteen.
