T07.2

Explain why a promising choice is best

Explain why balanced parts give the largest product, and handle restrictions.

2:00 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

A rectangle has positive whole-number side lengths in centimetres and a perimeter of 22 cm. What is the greatest area it can have?

Check your reasoning

The two side lengths add to 11. The possible shorter sides are 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5. Their areas are 10, 18, 24, 28 and 30 square centimetres. The 5 by 6 rectangle gives the maximum.

Read the transcript

0:00 Compare rectangles with the same fence

Take a fixed length of fence and make a rectangle. A long thin rectangle encloses very little. As the sides become more equal, the area grows. Balanced parts give bigger products.

0:13 Separate a pattern from a proof

You could discover this by trying examples. But in a competition you need to know it is always true, and what to do when perfect balance is not allowed.

0:23 Split twelve into two whole-number parts

Two positive whole numbers add to twelve. What is the largest their product can be? Pause and try a few pairs.

0:33 Compare the six possible products

One and eleven give eleven. Two and ten give twenty. Three and nine, twenty-seven. Four and eight, thirty-two. Five and seven, thirty-five. Six and six give thirty-six. The product climbs as the numbers get closer.

0:49 Identify the missing square of side d

Here is why. Write the two numbers as six minus d and six plus d. Start with a six by six square. Move a strip from one side to the other. The pieces no longer fit: a small square of side d is missing. So the product is thirty-six minus d squared. The further from balance, the more you lose.

1:10 Keep the two parts different

Now add a restriction: the two numbers must be different. Six and six is no longer allowed. Choose the most balanced pair that is allowed, five and seven, giving thirty-five.

1:22 Compare three fours with four threes

The number of parts matters too. Three fours give a product of sixty-four. Four threes give eighty-one. Both splits are balanced within their own number of parts, but the second product is bigger. Check how many parts the question actually allows.

1:39 Find the greatest area with 22 cm of fence

For two positive parts with a fixed total, the most balanced pair gives the largest product. When a rule forbids perfect balance, get as close as the rule allows. Notebook challenge: a rectangle with whole-number sides has perimeter twenty-two. What is its largest possible area?
