T17.3

Connect finite checks to general conclusions

Know when checking cases is a complete proof, and how to extend a check for ever.

2:11 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

With 4-cent and 5-cent stamps, what is the largest total that cannot be made?

Check your reasoning

11. Using 0, 1 or 2 five-cent stamps leaves 11, 6 or 1, none a multiple of 4. The totals 12, 13, 14 and 15 can all be made, and adding 4 repeatedly reaches everything larger.

Try a variation

With unlimited 3-cent and 4-cent stamps, prove that every whole amount from 6 cents upwards can be made.

See the worked answer

The consecutive totals 6, 7 and 8 have constructions 3+3, 3+4 and 4+4. Adding one 3-cent stamp to each construction gives the next block of three totals. Repeating covers every amount from 6 upwards.

Read the transcript

0:00 Decide when checking cases covers every possibility

Checking cases can be a complete proof, if there are only finitely many and you check them all. For endlessly many cases, checking is never enough on its own. But a check plus a rule that passes the result along can cover them all.

0:15 Identify a possible gap in a case proof

The danger with case checking is a missing case. A proof by cases must show that the cases cover everything, with nothing left outside.

0:24 Try the small totals with 3-cent and 5-cent stamps

You have plenty of three-cent and five-cent stamps. Which totals can you make exactly? Pause and test the totals from one to twelve.

0:36 List which totals up to twelve can be made

Three and five work. Six is two threes. Eight is three and five. Nine is three threes. Ten is two fives. Eleven is five and two threes. Twelve is four threes. But one, two, four and seven cannot be made.

0:52 Extend three consecutive totals by adding a stamp

Now the key step. Eight, nine and ten can all be made. Add a three-cent stamp to each and you get eleven, twelve and thirteen. Add another and you get fourteen, fifteen and sixteen. Three totals in a row, plus the ability to add three, reach every total from eight onwards.

1:11 Show why seven cents is impossible

Every total of eight or more is possible. Seven cannot be made. Two fives are too many. With one five we would need two more, and with no fives we would need a multiple of three. Neither works. So seven is the largest total that cannot be made.

1:29 Test all possible last digits of a square

A finite check can prove a never. Does any square number end in seven? The last digit of a square depends only on the last digit of the number. Check the ten digits: their squares end in zero, one, four, nine, six, five, six, nine, four and one. Seven never appears. Ten checks cover every whole number.

1:50 Find the last impossible total with 4-cent and 5-cent stamps

For finitely many cases, check them all and show nothing is left out. For endlessly many, check a starting block and give a rule that carries the result forward. Notebook challenge: with four-cent and five-cent stamps, what is the largest total you cannot make?
