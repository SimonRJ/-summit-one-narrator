T12.1

Build an angle chain from dependable facts

Find an unknown angle by linking dependable angle facts in a chain.

2:01 · Full HD

Try it in your notebook

An isosceles triangle has equal angles of 55 degrees. Find the third angle.

Check your reasoning

180 - 55 - 55 = 70 degrees.

Try a variation

An isosceles triangle has an exterior angle of 104° at its vertex between the equal sides. What are the base angles?

See the worked answer

The interior vertex angle is 180° − 104° = 76°. The base angles are equal and together make 104°, so each is 52°.

Read the transcript

0:00 Link facts about a straight line and a turn

Angle problems are solved by chains. You know one angle, a fact gives you the next, another fact gives the one after. The facts are few: a straight line makes one hundred and eighty degrees, a full turn makes three hundred and sixty, and angles opposite each other where two lines cross are equal.

0:19 Justify an angle without measuring

Competition diagrams may not be drawn to scale. Use the stated facts, a link at a time, and say which angle rule justifies each link.

0:29 Locate the marked angle below the 65° crossing

Two parallel lines are crossed by a third line. One angle at the top is sixty-five degrees. Find the marked angle at the bottom. Pause and trace how the top crossing relates to the bottom one.

0:43 Match corresponding angles

Because the lines are parallel, the top crossing is an exact copy of the bottom one. Slide it down and it fits. So the angle in the matching position at the bottom is also sixty-five degrees. These are called corresponding angles.

0:59 Use a straight line to get the marked angle

The angle we want sits next to that sixty-five on a straight line. Together they make one hundred and eighty. So the marked angle is one hundred and fifteen degrees. Two facts, two links, and the chain is complete.

1:13 Draw a parallel through a triangle’s vertex

The same parallel-line fact proves something famous. Draw a line through the top of a triangle, parallel to the base. The two base angles reappear beside the top angle, and the three sit together on a straight line. So the angles in any triangle add up to one hundred and eighty degrees.

1:31 Split the angles opposite equal sides

Add one more link for equal sides. In a triangle with two equal sides, the angles opposite them are equal. If the top angle is forty, the other two share one hundred and forty equally: seventy each.

1:45 Find the third angle from two 55° angles

Build a chain. Name the fact for each link. Notebook challenge: a triangle has two equal sides, and one of its two equal angles is fifty-five degrees. What is the third angle?
